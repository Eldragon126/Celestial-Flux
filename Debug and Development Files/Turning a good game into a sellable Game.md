\# VECTOR ANOMALY CODEX ROADMAP

\## Commercial Upgrade Roadmap For Turning A Good Game Into A Sellable Game



Goal:

Make VECTOR ANOMALY commercially stronger by focusing Codex work on the parts of the game that increase player retention, Steam wishlists, creator interest, replayability, and review quality.



Core Commercial Target:

VECTOR ANOMALY should not feel like “another neon arena shooter.” It should feel like:



> A physics roguelike where the player survives by weaponizing gravity, momentum, and collapsing spacetime.



Codex should prioritize systems that make the game:

\- instantly understandable

\- visually memorable

\- clip-worthy

\- replayable

\- readable during chaos

\- polished enough for a public demo

\- strong enough to support a Steam launch later



Do not add random features just because they sound cool. Every change should strengthen the core fantasy:



> Survive impossible physics. Master momentum. Turn gravity into a weapon.



\---



\# PHASE 1: SELLABLE CORE PASS



\## Objective



Make the first 10 minutes of VECTOR ANOMALY so strong that a new player immediately understands why the game is different.



The game already has decent fun, graphics, music, bosses, upgrades, and gravity systems. This phase is not about adding more bulk. This phase is about making the core experience sharper, faster, more readable, and more marketable.



\## Tasks



\- \[ ] Improve the first 60 seconds of gameplay so the player quickly experiences movement, dodging, shooting, slingshotting, and gravity interaction.

\- \[ ] Add or improve one early “holy cow” moment where gravity clearly affects enemies, bullets, the arena, or the player in a dramatic way.

\- \[ ] Make sure the player can understand the main gameplay fantasy within 3 seconds of seeing gameplay.

\- \[ ] Make the opening waves faster, cleaner, and more exciting.

\- \[ ] Remove or delay confusing mechanics that do not help the first 10 minutes.

\- \[ ] Make sure the first upgrade appears quickly and clearly changes behavior, not just stats.

\- \[ ] Improve controller aiming and movement feel.

\- \[ ] Make joystick aiming point the player in exact analog direction instead of only rotating clockwise/counterclockwise.

\- \[ ] Add strong visual/audio feedback when the player successfully uses gravity, orbiting, or momentum.

\- \[ ] Add stronger feedback for near misses, slingshots, orbit assists, and gravity kills.

\- \[ ] Improve enemy readability so players can tell what is dangerous within 1 second.

\- \[ ] Improve projectile readability during chaotic moments.

\- \[ ] Make the early game difficult enough to feel alive but not punishing before the player understands the controls.

\- \[ ] Add or refine tutorial prompts so they teach through action instead of long explanation.

\- \[ ] Make sure the first mini-boss or boss has a unique gravity-based identity.



\## Success Test



A new player should be able to play for 3 minutes and say something specific like:



> “The gravity thing is cool.”



Not just:



> “It seems pretty good.”

\# PHASE 2: CLIP-WORTHY GAMEPLAY SYSTEMS



\## Objective



Create moments that naturally market the game through short videos, screenshots, GIFs, and player stories.



VECTOR ANOMALY should constantly produce moments where the player barely survives, bends physics, destroys enemies through gravity, or watches reality collapse in a readable way.



\## Tasks



\- \[ ] Add or improve gravity kills where enemies can be destroyed by being pulled into planets, singularities, hazards, walls, or each other.

\- \[ ] Add orbit combos that reward controlled orbital movement.

\- \[ ] Add a clear multiplier, charge, or bonus for skillful momentum control.

\- \[ ] Add stronger visual language for successful slingshot maneuvers.

\- \[ ] Add a “near-death physics miracle” feedback event when the player survives by very small margins.

\- \[ ] Add reality collapse events that happen every few waves and visibly change the arena rules.

\- \[ ] Add named run modifiers such as Inverted Gravity, Time Debt, Volatile Moons, Echo Projectiles, Collapsing Arena, or similar.

\- \[ ] Make each run modifier visible in the UI and readable during gameplay.

\- \[ ] Make bosses alter the rules of the arena instead of only attacking the player.

\- \[ ] Make at least one boss bend, fracture, compress, rotate, or distort the arena.

\- \[ ] Add slow-motion or time dilation moments only when they improve readability and impact.

\- \[ ] Make explosions, trails, shockwaves, and collapse effects feel powerful without destroying performance.

\- \[ ] Add a replayable “spectacle moment” checklist and make sure each run can generate at least one.



\## Success Test



The game should naturally create moments that could be posted with captions like:



\- “I survived by slingshotting through a gravity well.”

\- “This boss bent the whole arena.”

\- “Gravity became the weapon.”

\- “The physics almost killed me with my own orbit.”

\# PHASE 3: READABLE CHAOS PASS



\## Objective



Make the game look intense without becoming visual soup.



The game should feel chaotic, but the player should still understand:

\- where they are

\- what is dangerous

\- what they caused

\- why they died

\- what they could do better next time



\## Tasks



\- \[ ] Audit all active visual effects for readability.

\- \[ ] Reduce or fade nonessential particles during high-danger moments.

\- \[ ] Make enemy projectiles visually distinct from player projectiles.

\- \[ ] Make lethal hazards visually distinct from decorative effects.

\- \[ ] Add priority layering so the player, enemy bullets, boss attacks, and danger zones remain readable.

\- \[ ] Add clearer warning telegraphs for boss attacks.

\- \[ ] Add clearer warning telegraphs for gravity collapses, time pockets, and arena hazards.

\- \[ ] Add color and shape rules for each major hazard type.

\- \[ ] Make the player silhouette visible even during heavy effects.

\- \[ ] Make the player trail useful for motion reading, not just decoration.

\- \[ ] Make UI warnings short, sharp, and readable.

\- \[ ] Add optional screen shake intensity settings.

\- \[ ] Add optional visual intensity settings for accessibility and performance.

\- \[ ] Add death recap text that explains the actual cause of death clearly.

\- \[ ] Make sure each death feels fair, even when the situation is intense.



\## Success Test



When the player dies, they should think:



> “I understand what happened. I can do better next time.”



Not:



> “I have no idea what hit me.”

\- \[ ] At least one reality collapse event.

\- \[ ] At least one mini-boss or boss.

\- \[ ] At least one gravity spectacle moment.

\- \[ ] Clear death screen.

\- \[ ] Restart flow.

\- \[ ] Wishlist reminder.

\- \[ ] Feedback link.

\- \[ ] Audio settings.

\- \[ ] Demo end screen that encourages wishlisting.

\- \[ ] No obvious debug visuals unless intentionally enabled.

\- \[ ] No broken controller flow.

\- \[ ] No major performance drops during normal gameplay.

\- \[ ] No unclear tutorial steps.

\- \[ ] No unreadable death moments.



\## Success Test

\- another run

\- the full game

\- to wishlist it

\- to show a clip to someone else

\- bullet hell

\- physics roguelike

\- gravity combat

\- orbit survival

\- collapsing simulation

\- momentum mastery

\- readable chaos

\# PHASE 6: REPLAYABILITY AND DEPTH PASS



\## Objective



Make the full game worth buying and replaying.



The demo sells the promise. The full game must deliver depth.



\## Tasks



\- \[ ] Add more build-defining upgrades.

\- \[ ] Remove or rework boring stat-only upgrades.

\- \[ ] Make upgrades change behavior whenever possible.

\- \[ ] Add upgrade synergies that create surprising but readable results.

\- \[ ] Add law modifiers that change how runs work.

\- \[ ] Add boss variants.

\- \[ ] Add enemy variants based on gravity, time, momentum, and resonance.

\- \[ ] Add challenge runs.

\- \[ ] Add unlockable difficulty modifiers.

\- \[ ] Add achievements.

\- \[ ] Add run history or post-run stats.

\- \[ ] Add score tracking if it supports replayability.

\- \[ ] Add optional seeded runs.

\- \[ ] Add a few rare events that players will talk about.

\- \[ ] Add late-game collapse systems that escalate without becoming unfair.

\- \[ ] Make each run feel different by wave 3–5.

\- \[ ] Make late-game builds visually and mechanically exciting.

\- \[ ] Make sure the strongest builds are powerful but not brainless.

\- \[ ] Make sure weak builds are improved or removed.



\## Success Test



A player should be able to play 5 runs and have each run feel meaningfully different.

\# PHASE 7: PERFORMANCE AND STABILITY PASS



\## Objective



Make the game smooth enough that polish, spectacle, and chaos do not collapse under performance problems.



The game can look intense, but performance must stay stable.



\## Tasks



\- \[ ] Profile CPU usage during normal waves.

\- \[ ] Profile CPU usage during boss fights.

\- \[ ] Profile CPU usage during heavy particle moments.

\- \[ ] Profile GPU usage during heavy visual effects.

\- \[ ] Identify expensive Polygon2D usage.

\- \[ ] Replace expensive procedural polygons with sprites or cached visuals where practical.

\- \[ ] Add visual effect budgets.

\- \[ ] Add particle budgets.

\- \[ ] Add enemy spawn budgets.

\- \[ ] Add projectile budgets.

\- \[ ] Add gravity source limits.

\- \[ ] Avoid global node searches inside physics ticks.

\- \[ ] Pool projectiles and temporary effects where useful.

\- \[ ] Pool frequent explosion or impact effects where useful.

\- \[ ] Add low-performance fallback settings.

\- \[ ] Add screen shake intensity settings.

\- \[ ] Add particle intensity settings.

\- \[ ] Add background intensity settings.

\- \[ ] Add debug performance overlay if not already present.

\- \[ ] Fix freed object errors.

\- \[ ] Fix invalid node reference errors.

\- \[ ] Fix controller menu bugs.

\- \[ ] Fix restart, game over, and title return bugs.

\- \[ ] Make sure the demo can run smoothly on lower-end computers.



\## Success Test



The game should remain smooth during the most marketable moments, because those are the moments players will record.

\# PHASE 8: PLAYTESTING AND FEEDBACK LOOP



\## Objective



Use real player behavior to improve the game instead of guessing.



Do not rely only on personal opinion. VECTOR ANOMALY needs strangers to test whether the hook actually lands.



\## Tasks



\- \[ ] Create a playtest build.

\- \[ ] Give the build to 10 trusted testers.

\- \[ ] Give the build to 25+ strangers after initial fixes.

\- \[ ] Watch where players get confused.

\- \[ ] Watch when players quit.

\- \[ ] Ask what moment they remember most.

\- \[ ] Ask what felt unfair.

\- \[ ] Ask what felt coolest.

\- \[ ] Ask what they would tell a friend about the game.

\- \[ ] Track how many players replay after death.

\- \[ ] Track how many players reach the first boss.

\- \[ ] Track how many players finish the demo.

\- \[ ] Improve the first 10 minutes based on feedback.

\- \[ ] Improve tutorial flow based on feedback.

\- \[ ] Improve visual clarity based on feedback.

\- \[ ] Improve upgrade choices based on feedback.

\- \[ ] Remove or rework mechanics that repeatedly confuse players.

\- \[ ] Keep mechanics that players naturally talk about.



\## Success Test



Players should describe the game using the intended fantasy:



> “It is a gravity roguelike where you use momentum and collapsing physics to survive.”



If they describe it as:



> “A space shooter thing.”



then the identity is not clear enough yet.

\# CODEX EXECUTION RULES



Codex should follow these rules while working on this roadmap:



\- Do not rewrite the entire project unnecessarily.

\- Do not remove core physics identity.

\- Do not flatten the game into a generic shooter.

\- Do not add random systems that are not connected to gravity, momentum, time, collapse, or replayability.

\- Do not create unreadable visual clutter.

\- Do not create upgrades that only increase numbers unless there is a strong reason.

\- Do not break controller support.

\- Do not break existing menus.

\- Do not break save/reset flow.

\- Do not leave half-finished systems.

\- Do not add placeholder content without marking it clearly.

\- Do not ignore performance.

\- Do not ignore readability.

\- Do not ignore the first 10 minutes.



Codex should prioritize:



1\. Player feel.

2\. First impression.

3\. Readability.

4\. Clip-worthy moments.

5\. Gravity-based identity.

6\. Replayability.

7\. Performance.

8\. Steam demo readiness.

9\. Full launch readiness.

\# DEFINITION OF DONE



A task is only complete if:



\- It works in-game.

\- It does not create obvious bugs.

\- It supports the core fantasy.

\- It is readable to the player.

\- It does not significantly hurt performance.

\- It is documented if needed.

\- It is tested enough to verify the change.

\- It does not make the game more generic.
