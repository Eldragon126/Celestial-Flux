My business advice for Vector Anomaly

You should treat the $100 Steam fee as a marketing tool, not just a hosting fee. You are not paying $100 just to upload a file. You are paying to unlock:

A public Steam page
Wishlists
A place to send players from TikTok, YouTube, Reddit, Discord, and festivals
A credibility badge that says, “This is a real game, not vapor-neon smoke” 🜂

Steam says a Coming Soon page lets players wishlist the game, and those players can receive launch notifications when the game releases. Steam also requires a Coming Soon page to be live for at least two weeks before release.

So yes, I think you should pay the $100 before the game is finished, but only when you have enough to make the page look real.

When you should make the Steam page

Make the Steam page when you have these 5 things:

1. A strong name and capsule art
You already have the name: VECTOR ANOMALY. That is good. It sounds sharp, weird, and marketable.

2. A clear 1-sentence hook
Example:

VECTOR ANOMALY is a physics-driven roguelike arena survival game where you weaponize gravity, momentum, and spacetime collapse to survive impossible orbital chaos.

3. At least 6 strong screenshots
Not random gameplay screenshots. You need screenshots that show:

Orbiting around gravity wells
Near-miss survival
Boss spectacle
Upgrade chaos
Clean readable UI
A big “what is happening? I want to play this” moment

4. A short trailer or GIF-quality footage
It does not need to be cinematic perfection yet. It does need to instantly show the hook. The first 5 seconds should scream:

“This is not just another neon arena shooter.”

5. A playable demo plan
You do not need the demo ready the same day the Steam page goes up, but you should know what the demo will contain.

Do not launch too early

Your Steam launch is a one-time cannon shot. Do not waste it with a “maybe people will like it” release.

The dangerous version is:

“I’ll just put it on Steam and see what happens.”

That usually becomes:

“Nobody saw it, nobody wishlisted it, and now launch day is gone.”

Steam says visibility is based on many factors, including player interest, so your job is to create interest before launch, not after.

Recommended path
Phase 1: Build the Steam page soon

Pay the $100 once you have a decent trailer/GIF, screenshots, capsule art, and description. The page can go up before the game is done.

Goal: start collecting wishlists.

Phase 2: Release a demo before the full game

For your game, a demo is extremely important because the hook is physical. Players need to feel the orbiting, slingshotting, and chaos. A good demo can do more marketing than a paragraph ever will.

Demo should include:

10-15 minutes of gameplay
1 polished arena
1 strong boss or mini-boss
A few upgrades that change behavior, not just stats
Enough juice that clips look insane
A “Wishlist now” button or end screen
Phase 3: Market through clips, not essays

Your game’s strength is not lore walls. It is visual chaos with skill. So market with short clips.

Best content types:

“I survived with 1 HP because gravity saved me”
“This boss broke the arena”
“I made a roguelike where momentum is your weapon”
“The map collapses if you survive too long”
“This upgrade bends bullets through gravity wells”

You want people to understand the game in 3 seconds.

Phase 4: Launch only when the game has proof

Before full release, you want signs like:

People are wishlisting without you begging
Demo players understand the hook
Some players replay the demo
Clips get comments like “what game is this?”
The first 10 minutes feel polished
The game has enough content to avoid bad reviews

Bad reviews at launch are revenue termites. They chew quietly and ruin the house.
